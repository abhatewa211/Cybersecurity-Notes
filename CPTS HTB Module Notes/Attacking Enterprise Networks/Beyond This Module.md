# 3. Review Weak Concepts

Once you've created your Concept Review List:

### Step 1

Identify the concepts where you're weak.

### Step 2

Use **Global Search** to find the relevant Academy sections.

### Step 3

Return to those sections.

### Step 4

Read the material again.

### Step 5

Perform the practical exercises again.

### Step 6

Try the concept without following the guide.

### Step 7

Repeat until you can perform the task independently.

# 4. Don't Just Re-Read — Practice

One of the biggest mistakes after completing a learning path is:

> Reading everything again without actually practicing.

For example, if you don't understand **Kerberoasting**, don't just reread the definition.

Instead:

```
Read
 ↓
Understand
 ↓
Set up / enter lab
 ↓
Enumerate
 ↓
Perform attack
 ↓
Capture evidence
 ↓
Explain attack
 ↓
Write remediation
```

The goal is **practical recall**, not recognition.

# 8. Academy vs Main Platform

### HTB Academy

Academy is primarily:

> **Guided learning**

You are taught:

```

 ↓

 ↓

 ↓

 ↓

 ↓

```

It provides structured guidance.

---

### HTB Main Platform

The main platform is more:

> **Challenge-based and self-guided**

You are given a target and expected to figure out:

```
What is running?
       ↓
What can I enumerate?
       ↓
What vulnerability exists?
       ↓
How can I exploit it?
       ↓
How can I escalate?
       ↓
How can I reach the objective?
```

You aren't necessarily told exactly what to do.

# 17. Pro Labs

**Pro Labs** are among the most realistic learning experiences HTB offers.

Pro Labs are designed around **Enterprise-grade IT environments**.

Instead of being given a simple vulnerable machine, you may need to approach the environment as you would an actual penetration test.

---

# 19. Pro Labs — Pentest Mindset

When working on a Pro Lab, don't think:

> "What trick does this machine expect?"

Think:

> **"If this were a real client network, how would I approach the assessment?"**

That means:

- Enumerate methodically.
- Document everything.
- Maintain an attack-path timeline.
- Avoid unnecessary destructive actions.
- Track credentials.
- Track hosts.
- Track access.
- Record evidence.
- Practice reporting.

This directly connects to the **Engagement Closeout** section you just studied.

# 25. Technical Skills Aren't Enough

The module emphasizes that technical knowledge isn't the only thing required to succeed as a penetration tester.

You also need **soft skills**.

The important ones listed are:

### Communication & Presentation

You must be able to explain:

> "What did I find?"

and:

> "Why does it matter?"

---

### Teamwork

Real penetration tests are usually performed as part of a team.

You may need to:

- Share findings
- Coordinate testing
- Discuss attack paths
- Perform handoffs
- Review other testers' work

---

### Writing

This is especially important for you because you're working on CPTS reporting.

You need to write:

- Technical findings
- Reports
- Emails
- Executive summaries
- Recommendations
- Resumes

---

### Emotional Intelligence

You may need to communicate difficult findings to clients without creating unnecessary conflict.

Example:

Instead of:

> "Your security is terrible."

Professional:

> "The assessment identified weaknesses in authentication controls that could allow an attacker to obtain unauthorized access."

Same problem.

Completely different communication.

---

### Courage

Penetration testers need the confidence to:

- Ask questions
- Report serious findings
- Explain technical risks
- Admit when they don't know something
- Investigate unfamiliar technologies

---

### Good Ethical Habits

This is critical.

You have access to potentially sensitive systems and information.

You must maintain:

- Confidentiality
- Integrity
- Professional boundaries
- Scope discipline
- Responsible handling of data
- Respect for authorization

# 27. Practice Explaining What You Learn

This is one of the best interview-preparation techniques.

Suppose you've learned **Kerberoasting**.

Don't just memorize:

> "Kerberoasting extracts service tickets."

Practice explaining:

### Beginner explanation

> Kerberoasting is an Active Directory attack where an attacker requests service tickets for accounts associated with SPNs and attempts to crack the ticket offline to recover the service account password.

### Interview explanation

Then explain:

- What it is
- Why it works
- Prerequisites
- What an attacker gains
- How to detect it
- How to mitigate it

Now you actually understand the topic.
